# Storylines

## A. The uncovered frame (chosen)

Device: a real brief and a real license, with the clearance strip as the spine.
Form: type cards cut against the real app. The viewer follows one 30-second Quiet Cut
quote from brief to refusal to a settled Morning Edit license and its public proof.

The first two seconds: deep green. The real strip appears. "Royalty free." lands above
an excluded segment.

Beats: hook -> reveal -> brief -> shortlist -> strip -> refusal -> settlement -> agent
verification -> stack -> end card.

Look: Grant's canvas and deep green, lemon as a fill, white product cards, Geist Mono
for chain data. Flat grounds, no glow.

Sound: a low CC0 bed from the demo catalogue, no voice. Large type carries the words.

## B. One license across apps

Device: a single license record travelling from quote to PDF to proof page.
Form: an object-to-object chain. The strip becomes the PDF header, the PDF becomes the
proof page, the proof page becomes the explorer row.

This is elegant but hides the human decision and the refusal, which are Grant's real
differences.

## C. The agent buys first

Device: the headless buyer.
Form: a terminal-led cold open. The agent reads the 402 challenge, refuses, then pays.

This proves the agent economy but makes Grant look like a developer tool. The editor's
story is the better lead. The agent beat stays in the film as proof, not as the hook.
