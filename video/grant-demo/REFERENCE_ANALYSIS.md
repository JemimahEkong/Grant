## Visual grammar

- Grounds: pure black for title cards, white for product and proof scenes.
- Type: giant left-aligned sans headlines with tight tracking. One line at a time.
- One accent: hot pink, used for section labels, underlines, PASS chips, values and the logo mark.
- Caption pill: black rounded rectangle, white semibold text, bottom centre, carries the narration.
- Product: real screenshots in rounded browser cards with soft shadows. No invented UI.
- Proof: real explorer cards, labelled with black pills. The labels do the explaining.
- Motion: title lines land and hold. Cards rise and settle. Diagrams draw in. No people, no stock footage, no particles.
- Sound: continuous narration and a music bed. The voice carries most of the story.

## What transfers

- The beat order: problem, reveal, mechanism, refusal, real proof, verification, stack, close.
- One idea per beat, one caption per beat, real product and real chain evidence.
- The contrast between full-frame typographic beats and floating real UI cards.
- The refusal and the "don't take our word for it" verification beats.

## What does not transfer

- The black and hot pink identity, the exact lines, the Rigbill logo and the NEAR claims.
- Mainnet language. Grant is a testnet product and must say so on screen.
- The narration voice itself. Grant's film is text-led unless a voice is recorded later.
